## Inaccuracies and tensions flagged in the notes

None of these is taught by any item. The author of the notes should consider reconciling them.

- **(a) Association of persons (s73).** Indira Balkrishna requires a common purpose or action "whose object is to produce income". The same slide says an association is a person "whether or not formed to earn income". On the better view, that statutory rule (from the 2002 Explanation to s2(31) of the 1961 Act) overtakes the income-object element of the case, at least on whether such a body is a person. Only the common-action and mere-joint-receipt parts are safe to teach without qualification.
- **(b) Partial integration (s68).** The slide gives the 1961-regime method and says "confirm in the current Finance Act". It does not say whether the method applies to persons taxed under s202. No item depends on either point.
- **(c) Kinariwala (s55) and s76.** "The whole share was taxable in his hands" was the result for years before a partner's share of firm profits became exempt (now Schedule III, s76). The case stands today only for application versus diversion.
- **State Bank of Travancore (s54).** The slide does not mention the later statutory credit-or-receipt rule for interest on bad or doubtful debts of banks and financial institutions (s43D of the 1961 Act). The notes do not say whether, or where, the 2025 Act carries it forward. Verify and add a line.
- **Toshoku (s58).** The holding assumes the commission is not fees for technical services. The point is arguable: after the CBDT withdrew its circular on non-resident agents' commission, the Revenue has at times treated such commission as fees for technical services. A one-line caveat would help.
- **Rates (s69).** The slide says s4 charges at rates "enacted by a Central Act (the Finance Act)", but the new-regime slabs sit in s202 of the Act itself. No item asks where the rates come from.
- **Minor (s52).** "Special allowances for official duties to the extent actually spent" are listed as receipts that are not income. Strictly, such allowances fall within the definition of income (s2(49)) and are exempt to the extent spent. A pure reimbursement of expense incurred for the employer is not income at all. q07 uses a pure reimbursement, so it is unaffected.
